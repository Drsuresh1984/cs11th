1.2 History of Computers:

In ancient times, people used simple tools like pebbles, animal bones, and even their fingers to count and do basic math. As time passed, people invented different devices to make counting easier and faster.So, the history of computers is the story of how humans created different tools and machines to help with counting and calculating, from simple objects to advanced electronic devices.Here are some important devices that are part of the history of computers:

(a) Abacus: It was developed by China. It is considered as the first computing device. In its imagestandard form it can be used to perform addition, subtraction , division and multiplication. Frame, rod and beads are its basic components. For counting and calculation we slide small beads along rods according to set procedure.

image

(b) Napier's Bone: It was an advanced version of the basic abacus. This calculating device is named after its inventor, John Napier, who is well-known for developing logarithms. The device consisted of a set of eleven rods called Bones because they were originally carved from bones. Numbers were engraved on these rods. This tool was especially useful for performing multiplication and division of large numbers.

image

(c) Slide Rule: The slide rule was invented in 1620 by William Oughtred. It has scales with marks that show the logarithms of numbers. It helps to do calculations with powers, trigonometric functions, and more. Even today, engineering students use the slide rule.

image

(d) Pascal Mechanical Calculator: Blaise Pascal in 1642 invented the first adding machine called the Pascaline. It was a rectangular brass box with eight movable dials that could add numbers up to eight digits long. It could perform all four basic arithmetic operations faster than earlier calculating device

(e) Leibniz Mechanical Multiplier: Leibniz developed a imagecalculating machine that could directly multiply and divide numbers. It was the first machine capable of performing these operations without repeated addition or subtraction. This device was an improved version of the Pascaline. .

(f) Charles Babbage's Differential and

Analytical Engine: Charles Babbage is known as the Father of Modern Computers. He developed a model of a mechanical computer called the Difference Engine, which could accurately solve algebraic expressions and create mathematical tables up to 20 decimal places. Later, he designed another machine called the Analytical Engine. This was an automatic computing machine that could perform additions at a speed of 60 per minute and also had imagememory.

(g) ENIAC (1945): ENIAC stands for Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer.The first general-purpose electronic digital computer, capable of performing complex calculations faster than any machine before imageit.

(h) UNIVAC (1951): UNIVAC Stands for Universal Automatic Computer. The first commercially produced computer used for business and government purposes.

(i) Personal Computers (1970s onwards):These are small and affordable computers for everyday use, including desktops and laptops.
