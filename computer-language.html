Concepts of computer Languages:

Computer Language:

image

       A computer language serves as a medium of communication between the user and the computer system. It consists of a series of instructions written in a particular format, known as coding, which directs the computer to perform specific tasks. Computer languages are primarily categorized into two types: Low-Level Languages (like Machine Language) and High-Level Languages (like C, C++).

Low Level Languages:

       A low-level programming language communicates directly with a computer's hardware. As a result, programs written in these languages are machine-dependent and cannot be easily transferred between different systems. These languages do not require a compiler or interpreter for translation, as they are already in or close to machine code. Programs developed in low-level languages are known for their high speed and efficient memory usage. However, they are difficult to write, read, and maintain. Low-level languages are commonly used for developing operating systems, device drivers, and other applications that need direct hardware interaction. They are further divided into two categories: Machine Language and Assembly Language.

High Level Languages:

       High-level programming languages are machine-independent and are designed to be easy to write, read, edit, and understand. To execute programs written in these languages, translators such as compilers or interpreters are used to convert the high-level code into machine code. Examples of high-level languages include Java, Pascal, COBOL, C++, and C.
